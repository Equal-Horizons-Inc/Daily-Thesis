Privacy Policy

Last updated: July 19, 2026

Daily Thesis is built to keep your reading experience local and minimal wherever possible. This page explains what the app stores on your device, what it sends over the network, what third-party services it relies on, and how you can ask us to delete data we hold.

What Daily Thesis stores on your device only

The app stores the following locally so it can personalize recommendations and remember your preferences. None of this leaves your device.

  • Your name, if you enter it during onboarding or in Settings.
  • Your selected subjects and matched interest categories.
  • Your theme preference (System, Light, or Dark) and text size.
  • Papers you've liked, and papers you've skipped or marked "not interested in," so recommendations improve over time.
  • The local recommendation events and learned ranking information derived from those likes and dismissals.
  • Your paper history, so you can revisit papers you've previously been shown.

Uninstalling the app permanently deletes all of the above, since none of it is copied to a server.

Getting your daily paper

The app requests a paper recommendation from its backend service, which aggregates from arXiv, PubMed, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, ChemRxiv, bioRxiv, and medRxiv. This request includes your selected subject only — no account or persistent identifier is attached to it.

When you tap "Read Full Publication," "Cite," or "Share," the app opens external links or system share sheets using your device's own apps and services.

Push notifications

If you allow notifications, the app generates a random device identifier (not tied to your name, email, or any account) and registers it, along with your device's notification token and selected subjects, with our backend so we know where and what to send your daily notification. This is separate from the anonymous paper-fetching request described above, and is the only place a persistent identifier is sent off your device.

This data is transmitted securely (HTTPS) and stored only for as long as needed to deliver notifications. See "Requesting data deletion" below to have it removed.

Analytics

Daily Thesis uses Firebase Analytics (a Google service) to understand how the app is used and to fix problems. This includes app opens, screen views, which subjects you select (as counts, not tied to your name), whether you liked, skipped, or disliked a paper (recorded as a subject and action only — never which specific paper), and whether a paper was served from the offline cache. Firebase also automatically collects basic device and diagnostic information (device model, OS version, app version) and an approximate, IP-derived location. Google processes this data on our behalf as our analytics provider; it is not sold or shared with other companies. See Google's Privacy Policy for details on how Google handles this data.

Advertising

Daily Thesis does not currently show ads. Ad support (Google AdMob) is built into the app but disabled. If we turn ads on in the future, this policy will be updated first to describe what that involves, and the change will also be reflected in the app's Play Store data safety disclosure.

Discuss with ChatGPT

If you tap "Discuss with ChatGPT" on a paper, the app opens ChatGPT with that paper's title, authors, and abstract pre-filled into a prompt for you to review and send. This is publicly available information about the paper itself, not personal data about you. The app does not send anything to OpenAI unless you tap this button, and the request happens directly between your device and ChatGPT — Daily Thesis is not in the middle of it. Use of ChatGPT is subject to OpenAI's Privacy Policy.

What Daily Thesis does not do

  • It does not require an account or login.
  • It does not sell your personal information to anyone.
  • It does not store your reading history, liked papers, name, or preferences on a server — those stay on your device only.
  • It does not attach your name or any local preference to the notification identifier or analytics data described above.

Requesting data deletion

The only data Daily Thesis stores off your device is the notification registration described above (a random device identifier, notification token, and selected subjects). To request deletion of this data, email us at the address below with enough detail for us to locate your record (for example, the approximate date you installed the app). We'll confirm once it's deleted.

Locally stored data (name, history, liked papers, preferences) is entirely in your control — clearing the app's storage or uninstalling it removes this data immediately, without needing to contact us.

Children's privacy

Daily Thesis is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact us using the details below and we'll remove it.

Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated to reflect product or legal changes. The current version will always be published at this URL, with the "Last updated" date above reflecting the most recent revision.
